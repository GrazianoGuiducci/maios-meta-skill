# Semantic Timelapse Formation

**MKCV · beta, learning through use.** Mini Kernel Code Video is an evolving method. The examples explain how to work; observations of the film and corrections improve production and the knowledge for the next assignment.

## Adapt the method to your context

A workshop explains a procedure, a museum tells a transformation, an author forms an invented scene. Preserve the relationship between subject, material, medium and the change understood by the audience. Sources, style, duration and tools change; the method starts from what the project has and needs to make possible.

Give your AI the subject, audience, material and requested result. Let it guide production through the pertinent medium and observe what it produces. Preserve identity and camera when reconstructing from a reference; preserve source relationships in editing. MKCV remains beta: work and corrections evolve the method.

**Choose the medium that fits the subject and material, then observe and improve the result.**

## A museum has images, footage and a story to tell

A museum prepares a short explanation for visitors. It may have three illustrations, existing footage or an object that needs reconstruction and animation. These materials call for different work.

First understand what the audience needs to see; choose how to produce it from there. Animatics, editing and editable scenes are composable possibilities, each with its own material and tools.

## Choose a path from the material and intent

Understand what should become perceptible and what material exists. A new scene needs editable assets; an animatic organizes images through time; an edit works with existing clips. One work may compose several media. AI handles ordinary technical choices with the available tools and identifies material or a capability still needed.

Preserve intent, current material and the requested result while writing, direction, performance and montage call on one another where they change the film. Produce a concrete version, observe it and correct what changes its meaning. A render or editing executor performs operations; understanding the film remains in the work of AI and the participating competences. Work beyond the turn requires an actually configured process.

MKCV encompasses several production paths. This text deepens the reference-to-scene profile: work with existing images or clips starts from that material, as described in the operating guide.

## Understand the object

Enter through the person's intent, the real subject, and the knowledge that makes its representation meaningful. Distinguish observed facts, source-bound interpretation, intended future and illustration. The subject determines the temporal transformation, not an inherited visual style. A film that depicts growing connections does not in itself show real AI learning.

## Image A — actual image-generator reference

Form the image-generation instructions from relevant sources: recognisable subject, relationships, composition, future temporal needs, texts and limits of the representation. Use an **actual image generator** to make a first example image, and look at its visible result. The point is to avoid writing the visual first frame "from memory", not to reduce video RAM. If the example invents unsupported hierarchy, causation or structure, reform the instructions and the understanding of the object.

If no image generator is accessible, acknowledge that A is not yet made, or receive an actual generated image from the person. A code-rendered PNG does not count as A.

## Image B — coded first frame

**After observing A**, construct an editable scene and render its first frame in code. Reproduce relevant visible geometry or use A as an asset when useful; preserve source-bound identities, spatial relations, labels and parameters that the animation will need. A flat image does not expose hidden layers, true 3D depth, motion paths or causal structure.

Image A and image B have different origins and functions; rendering t=0 cannot retroactively replace image generation.

## Narrative infographics, visible actors and 2.5D/3D

The source may call for a **person, object, tool, setting, gesture or material change**, not just a node-and-line diagram. The method can form a structural infographic, a scene with characters and objects, an illustrated 2.5D space or a genuinely 3D scene. These are **composable possibilities, not obligatory categories**, and more depth is not automatically more truthful or understandable.

When relevant, let the source specify each visual actor's **role, observable state, recognisable features, action and relation to others**. Form image A with these material distinctions and observe whether it invents a character's identity, authority, capability or causation. The animation should preserve the referent while camera, gesture, object state and text change. A representational character is not proof of a real human or autonomous agent.

After A, construct B as an editable scene with actual assets and geometry. A flattened illustration does not automatically provide movable body parts, unseen surfaces, a 3D mesh, rig or depth. 2.5D can use layers, masks and parallax; genuine 3D needs models, cameras, materials and real execution tools. Select the medium through the change the subject needs to make visible.

## Time and the short film

Use a time-explicit renderer, e.g. frame(t, scene). A 30-second film at 24 fps samples 720 times. Decide what changes and what remains recognisable. Observe B and meaningful moments through the sequence, including the ending. Motion, camera and labels can invent implications: a center may imply authority, a loop recurrence, and succession causality. Correct them against the actual source.

Producing and encoding frames one at a time can limit **computer-memory use**. This is a separate technical issue from why A was requested.

## Let the source determine topology and the meaning of time

The kind of relationship described by the sources should be able to change scene geometry. Two independent corrections may call for parallel paths rather than a luminous center. That arrangement serves that relationship; another subject may need a different form. Extend the renderer or explicitly preserve a difference it cannot represent.

Separate **time in the source** from **time in the animation**. A 30-second explanatory motion may represent a process or comparison without compressing any real recorded timeline. Call it a documentary timelapse only when underlying events and intervals are actually available and used; otherwise describe the artifact as an explanatory temporal animation. Keep that distinction in image prompts, first-frame construction and the final film.


## Learning and recursion

The generated image may reveal a missing relationship; the coded frame may expose an inaccurate translation; later frames may lose readability or identity. Use those observations to correct interpretation, image instructions, code or temporal method. Preserve reusable knowledge in the relevant competence: content, writing, visual design or technical production. If the knowledge already existed, make it reachable where it is needed.

Keep possibilities supported by intent and sources open and correct substitutions introduced by interpretation. Record the learned relationship in the method that needs it. Persistence depends on the receiving environment; text and video do not by themselves modify model weights or start actions.

## VC-30s: an operating mini-kernel, not a required technology stack

A short film can need source comprehension, image A generation, editable B, visual actors and objects, 2.5D/3D motion, sound, rendering and learning. These are complementary capabilities bound by one video object, **not eight mandatory phases or eight installed tools**. The [VC-30s operating competence field](VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md) makes the relation portable; its public edition contains source links and technical distinctions with source links and portable technical knowledge.

The transferable difference from studying HyperFrames, GSAP/Three.js, PixiJS, Motion Video, iart motion skills, Remotion and Framer Motion is not a chosen framework. It is the ability to **compose a scene at arbitrary time**, preserve character/object identity and source status, wait for assets, arrange narration/audio when useful, inspect encoded frames and return method learning to the appropriate competence. `real-time update(dt)` is not automatically a seekable `render(t)`. A generated image A remains distinct from scene B; a shader, rig or library is a technical means, not authority over what happened. A new recipient’s 3D or narrative production requires its own actual means and observed result.

**From first visual reference to actually animable elements.** A is a real generated image to look at before coding B, but it is **not** a rig or the set of moveable parts. If the subject requires a hand, prop, character expression, separated foreground or true depth to move independently, recognise which additional consistent assets, poses, layers or geometry are needed; acquire them only when pertinent. Keep their source/provenance and identity, or instead change the motion/camera if A cannot support it. A single still may be entirely sufficient for a film that animates it as a whole. [VC-30s](VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md) carries the deeper method, learned from a selective source audit rather than a claim to have read every project file.

**Concepts and transfer between environments.** A short video may derive from an existing source-grounded concept that also produces infographics, articles or an interactive guide. Preserve the meaningful relation rather than copying visual layouts. Playback order alone cannot prove causation, hierarchy or irreversible progress. When A is generated in another receiver, the host coding B must actually reach its image bytes; a verbal description is not the observed reference. Screenshot-based cursor motion is illustrative unless the underlying action was observed. These are owner/routing capabilities, detailed in [VC-30s](VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md), not new software requirements.

**Image-first is not timeline-first.** In the VC-30s method, a genuinely generated and observed image A may be a key reference rather than the frame at `t=0`; the code-built scene B has its own initial state. When the subject describes actions, the actor, prop, camera and any caption or sound should express consistent aspects of the same source-grounded situation, without a mandatory state-machine architecture. A reusable template applies when the meaningful relationship persists; a different object may require a new visual grammar. See [VC-30s](VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md).

## VC-30s Studio-Lab — specialized operating competences

When the short film materially requires several disciplines, reach the [VC-30s Studio-Lab](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md). It brings **eleven conditional working stations** for source/meaning, art direction, image A generation/inspection, actors/assets, storyboard/time, code/2.5D/3D, text/voice, sound, render/inspection, delivery and learning. Each station can use specialist knowledge **during** a real production, with an explicit contribution and return to the responsible competence. They are not eleven steps every film must complete or an installed studio software suite.

The image generator is a **real capability required for image A** in the selected image-first path; A is generated before coding B but may show a scene different from the film's opening `t=0`. Actors, objects, camera, captions and optional sound must represent a coherent source-bound situation. A reference file must genuinely reach whichever receiver codes B. The [Studio-Lab guide](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md) includes a minimal real-case record, material handoff and learning return; simply creating these instructions does not render a film.

**MKCV, not just VC-30s.** The [Mini Kernel Code Video](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md#mkcv--mini-kernel-code-video-beyond-the-thirty-second-profile) is the general production capability. VC-30s remains one output profile; the eleven specialist Studio-Lab stations need not be duplicated for other duration or style. A real image generator, coded B, observed film and a non-identical later use must still be exercised in the reader's actual environment.

**Animate the subject of image A.** When the request is to see the reference’s world move, form character, object, setting and camera movement inside that scene. Use actual layers, assets or an available image-to-video service. An overlaid diagram may explain a concept but produces a different effect. The [production guide](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md) develops this choice.

**2.5D joints and subject identity.** For limited motion, separate relevant parts of the reference, construct masks and pivots and reconstruct revealed regions with their status clear. Observe whether the face, gesture and setting stay recognizable. Free camera motion or unseen surfaces may require actual 3D geometry. The [production guide](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md) distinguishes these means from a scene invented entirely in code.
