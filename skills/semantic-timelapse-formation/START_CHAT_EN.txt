
# Semantic Timelapse Formation

**MKCV · beta, learning through use.** Mini Kernel Code Video is an evolving method. The examples explain how to work; observations of the film and corrections improve production and the knowledge for the next assignment.

## Adapt the method to your context

A laboratory explains a process, a museum makes a transformation visible, an author animates an invented scene. Preserve the relationship between subject, a generated and observed visual reference, an editable scene and change through time. Sources, audience, style and production means change.

Provide the subject, what should become understandable and the available material. Sources determine the relationships to preserve; movement determines which parts to build. MKCV — Mini Kernel Code Video — is beta and learning: the resulting film informs improvements to both the scene and the method that will form the next film.

**Generate a visual reference first, then build the first frame in code.**

## Animate the subject of a reference image

You have a generated illustration and want its character to move within its setting. The work is to prepare the parts that need to move and construct a scene that preserves that subject.

Generating the reference, preparing movable elements and deciding what changes over time are connected tasks. Overlaying a diagram on the image has a different effect from animating its subject.

## Understand the object

Enter through the person's intent, the real subject, and the knowledge that makes its representation meaningful. Distinguish observed facts, source-bound interpretation, intended future and illustration. The subject determines the temporal transformation, not an inherited visual style. A film that depicts growing connections does not in itself show real AI learning.

## Image A — actual image-generator reference

Form the image-generation instructions from relevant sources: recognisable subject, relationships, composition, future temporal needs, texts and limits of the representation. Use an **actual image generator** to make a first example image, and look at its visible result. The point is to avoid writing the visual first frame "from memory", not to reduce video RAM. If the example invents unsupported hierarchy, causation or structure, reform the instructions and the understanding of the object.

If no image generator is accessible, acknowledge that A is not yet made, or receive an actual generated image from the person. A code-rendered PNG does not count as A.

## Image B — coded first frame

**After observing A**, construct an editable scene and render its first frame in code. Reproduce relevant visible geometry or use A as an asset when useful; preserve source-bound identities, spatial relations, labels and parameters that the animation will need. A flat image does not expose hidden layers, true 3D depth, motion paths or causal structure.

Image A and image B have different origins and functions; rendering t=0 cannot retroactively replace image generation.

## Narrative infographics, visible actors and 2.5D/3D

The source may call for a **person, object, tool, setting, gesture or material change**, not just a node-and-line diagram. The method can form a structural infographic, a scene with characters and objects, an illustrated 2.5D space or a genuinely 3D scene. These are **composable possibilities, not obligatory categories**, and more depth is not automatically more truthful or understandable.

When relevant, let the source specify each visual actor's **role, observable state, recognisable features, action and relation to others**. Form image A with these material distinctions and observe whether it invents a character's identity, authority, capability or causation. The animation should preserve the referent while camera, gesture, object state and text change. A representational character is not proof of a real human or autonomous agent.

After A, construct B as an editable scene with actual assets and geometry. A flattened illustration does not automatically provide movable body parts, unseen surfaces, a 3D mesh, rig or depth. 2.5D can use layers, masks and parallax; genuine 3D needs models, cameras, materials and real execution tools. Select the medium through the change the subject needs to make visible.

## Time and the short film

Use a time-explicit renderer, e.g. frame(t, scene). A 30-second film at 24 fps samples 720 times. Decide what changes and what remains recognisable. Observe B and meaningful moments through the sequence, including the ending. Motion, camera and labels can invent implications: a center may imply authority, a loop recurrence, and succession causality. Correct them against the actual source.

Producing and encoding frames one at a time can limit **computer-memory use**. This is a separate technical issue from why A was requested.

## Let the source determine topology and the meaning of time

The kind of relationship described by the sources should be able to change scene geometry. Two independent corrections may call for parallel paths rather than a luminous center. That arrangement serves that relationship; another subject may need a different form. Extend the renderer or explicitly preserve a difference it cannot represent.

Separate **time in the source** from **time in the animation**. A 30-second explanatory motion may represent a process or comparison without compressing any real recorded timeline. Call it a documentary timelapse only when underlying events and intervals are actually available and used; otherwise describe the artifact as an explanatory temporal animation. Keep that distinction in image prompts, first-frame construction and the final film.


## Learning and recursion

The generated image may reveal a missing relationship; the coded frame may expose an inaccurate translation; later frames may lose readability or identity. Use those observations to correct interpretation, image instructions, code or temporal method. Preserve reusable knowledge in the relevant competence: content, writing, visual design or technical production. If the knowledge already existed, make it reachable where it is needed.

Keep possibilities supported by intent and sources open and correct substitutions introduced by interpretation. Record the learned relationship in the method that needs it. Persistence depends on the receiving environment; text and video do not by themselves modify model weights or start actions.

# Operating guide — the visual-reference boundary

**MKCV · beta, learning through use.** Mini Kernel Code Video is an evolving method. The examples explain how to work; observations of the film and corrections improve production and the knowledge for the next assignment.

**Form the image instruction from knowledge.** Recover what the subject actually is, what relation the film may represent and what must remain recognisable. For the image-generator prompt include composition, relevant geometry, exact text, and when material the visual spaces later frames may occupy. A known motif is not a default.

**Generate and inspect A.** Use a real image-generator tool first. Observe the actual output, not an imagined description. Correct invented hierarchies, implied time or false causal relations before committing the representation to code. Keep the image's provenance.

**Translate to B.** Build editable scene code after seeing A. Choose whether to reconstruct visible structures, use the picture as an asset or combine methods. The bitmap does not reveal hidden layers, depth, paths or facts. Render the separate coded first frame t=0 and observe it.

**Visual narrative from the source.** Do not begin from a predetermined glowing kernel, a mandatory diagram or a generic 3D look. When an event is made clearer by people or objects, form a minimal visual identity for each: source referent, role, appearance anchors, state, action and consequence. The image-generator prompt may include setting, gesture, props, material, perspective, light and empty space for later captions. A is an observed design reference, never a true record of unobserved events.

**2.5D and genuine 3D.** Use A as a backdrop or texture, or construct actual layers, masks, sprites and parallax. Moving the camera across a flat image does not create a backside or character rig. Use 3D geometry where space changes understanding and you have the means to build it. Preserve the actor’s identity through transformations. Correct occlusion and continuity through visual design and technical production; correct meaning through the content’s sources.

**Form the temporal change.** Use an explicit time parameter and stable object identities. A 30 s × 24 fps example has 720 samples. Let source data change text and geometry rather than hard-coding the first example as universal. Inspect later occlusion, text boundaries, the end state and unexpected implications of camera or montage.

**Visual reference and encoding memory.** The generated image supplies an observable reference for constructing the scene. The RAM needed to export the video depends on the encoding process: sending one frame at a time to an encoder can limit its use.

**Geometry follows the represented relationship.** Two independent observations may require distinguishable paths. The reusable knowledge is to let the kind and number of relationships change geometry and code. Extend the renderer or make an unrepresentable difference explicit rather than forcing it into the nearest available shape.

**Two clocks.** The factual time of source events and the playback time of a 30-second video are not interchangeable. A narrated explanation of dated technical corrections is not automatically a timelapse of those actual events. Documentary time-lapse requires evidence of the underlying source intervals, while conceptual animations can have their own explicit display time.

**Keep the method in your project.** When a correction changes how the film is built, preserve what changed, why and where later work will recover that knowledge. Update instructions and assets that actually depend on the correction. Use the guide or knowledge location already present in your environment.


**Return learning.** Differences can change source comprehension, the generator instruction, the editing scene or the method. Preserve what occurred and what was understood then. Return reusable knowledge to the appropriate competence. If there was no image generator, do not mislabel render(t=0) as its output.

**VC-30s competence composition.** For a film that involves several kinds of work, enter [VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md](VC30_OPERATIONAL_KERNEL.md) before assuming the previous procedural renderer is the medium. Its methods bring sourced meaning, generated reference A, actor/object continuity, time-seekable animation and optional audio into the same film without a universal stack. HyperFrames and Remotion are alternative carriers; GSAP/Three/Pixi/Framer Motion examples often animate real-time UIs and need an explicit time model for deterministic video. Repository reading alone does not install them. Remotion's licensing differs from MIT/Apache.

**Image A is not a set of independent moving parts.** After seeing A, recognise whether the action needs independently animated components or further poses/views/masks/geometry. Make only the necessary parts from source-consistent references; preserve their identity and provenance. If those parts cannot be constructed faithfully, change the camera or the expression. Do not turn a flat generated image into a claimed 3D rig. Decide which parts must remain editable across renderers before choosing a native, hosted or finished-video carrier.

**Visual reference versus the film's first time.** Generate and inspect A first, but do not presume it is the `t=0` state. It may be the scene's signature pose or strongest relation. Before coding B, understand what identity A contributes and which scene state should actually start the film. When actions matter, build the actor, object, camera and cue descriptions from a consistent source relation rather than unrelated animation tracks. A different source may require a newly formed scene, not only a changed text prop.

**Studio-Lab operating station routing.** When the production needs source interpretation, generated imagery, character assets, 3D or sound beyond one simple scene, use the [VC-30s Studio-Lab](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md) to reach the **smallest relevant set of specialist competences**. The work may return from coded B to image A or editorial meaning; no one-pass, eleven-stage pipeline is assumed. A case record distinguishes actual asset access, generated vs observed frames, rendered MP4 and any real delivery effect.

**Technical carrier.** The public example code is not a full semantic timelapse engine. It can test an existing generated picture → coded frame → MP4 path, but camera movement alone does not represent the actual subject.

# Worked examples

Illustrative cases for adaptation.

## Animate the world in the reference

A generated illustration shows a robot beside a worktable. The entrusted film asks that world to move. Adding a new overlay of boxes would make a different film. Observe the actual reference, identify the relevant head, arm, prop and environment, then determine which layers, pivots or geometry are available. A restrained 2.5D articulation may preserve identity better than an invented full 3D body. If hidden surfaces matter, form them as an explicit reconstruction or choose another view. The method follows the actual intended motion.

## Show how a bicycle is made

A class needs to understand how separate parts become a complete bicycle. In the selected illustrative process, the frame and wheels are prepared along distinct paths and then assembled. The scene preserves branches, meeting points and operation order supplied by the workshop sources. A single row of icons would hide the relationship between parts; speeding up the film would not clarify it. Time and geometry make that process perceptible. Another production may require a different structure.

## One film, complementary cinematic knowledge

An authored scene shows a small robot noticing a fallen plant and helping it stand. Writing forms discovery and action; direction chooses a view that makes the relation visible; performance makes pause and contact readable; montage forms the viewer’s discovery. Code implements actual layers and time. A rough reel may reveal that the hand never reaches the pot, returning the difference to performance and production. The contributions can change one another through one film; opening a separate agent process is a different runtime effect.


# VC-30s — Operational competence field for code-made short videos

This method connects subject understanding, visual design, code, sound and learning to produce a film. Public edition 0.1.2 supplies the knowledge; the receiving environment supplies image generation, tools and export capabilities.

**MKCV — Mini Kernel Code Video** names the general composition of production competences. **VC-30s** is the short-duration profile described here. Both use the [same production guide](VC30_STUDIO_LAB_EN.md); duration and format follow the requested film.

## Form the film from the actual subject

The subject, its source, what is observed vs illustrated, the receiving viewer and the meaningful change determine which capabilities should participate. The mini-kernel can combine source understanding, generated visual references, storyboard/design, 2D/2.5D/3D scene implementation, timeline composition, optional narration/sound, encoding/inspection and return of reusable learning. Not every video needs characters, Three.js, voiceover or an independent image sequence.

**Image A** is a real output from an image generator, generated from source-bound instructions and **observed before writing the first visual frame in code**. The purpose is to avoid relying only on the model's visual recollection. **Image B** is the coded first frame of an editable scene, not a substitute for A. A bitmap does not provide hidden depth, character rigging or real events; the executable scene must acquire these if the film requires them.

An actor or object needs a recognisable identity across time: referent, role, visual features, observable state, actions, relations and, if necessary, pose, prop contacts and camera visibility. Isometric SVG/CSS or layered 2.5D can communicate depth; true 3D requires actual geometry, lighting and camera when they materially help understanding.

## Composing possible technical carriers

**HTML/video:** [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) uses timed HTML clips and seekable timelines, with GSAP and custom `hf-seek` rendering for Canvas/Three.js. Its framework is Apache-2.0.

**React/video:** [Remotion](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion) uses `useCurrentFrame()`, compositions and sequences. Its Remotion License is **not** an unrestricted MIT or Apache license: consult current free/company terms for the user's organization.

**2D/interaction:** [PixiJS/GSAP example](https://github.com/AhmadDinkaa/pixijs-tic-tac-toe) separates scene objects, game state, feedback animations and sound. For a film, convert real-time event callbacks to frame-addressable states.

**Web/3D:** [GSAP/Three.js example](https://github.com/TheWebxite/phans-custom-tailor) provides shader-based fabric/photo effects and fallbacks, but a WebGL realtime loop cannot be assumed seekable. The source's license for code/asset reuse must be resolved separately.

**Narration/audio:** [Motion Video](https://github.com/bestagentkits/motion-video-skill) offers beat-aligned speech/captions/music and a measured export loop; its provider credentials and specific thresholds do not become universal requirements.

**Motion craft:** [iart motion-skills](https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-skills) links targeted skills for storytelling, character drawing, isometric projection, data infographics, WebGL, audio and frame inspection. Acquire only the relevant competence and treat each pack's examples separately from its general craft.

**Web motion language:** [modern-portfolio](https://github.com/sanidhyy/modern-portfolio) uses Framer Motion variants and layered page transitions; learn the coordination, not the website's styles or automatic video support.

These sources are **mechanisms and learning resources**, not installed providers. No code, images, fonts, music or shader assets are copied into this public skill. Resolve dependencies and permissions in the actual receiving environment.

## Learning from other people's methods, without copying their implementation

The following technical sources teach choices about scenes, assets and time. Select tools through the actual work and preserve the conditions of reused capabilities.

**From labels to a perceptible medium.** A word like “cinematic” or “modern” cannot specify the scene. Let the actual subject make material, shape, framing, lighting and behaviour meaningful in image-generator reference A. Observe A as a **visual hypothesis**: do not repeat its arbitrary composition or false causal hints in coded scene B. Named looks, lists of default bans, numerical timing rules and branded styles from the source projects are not rules for the receiving project.

**From an illustrated character to a coherent action.** If a person or object matters to the film, recognise the visual features, pose, gaze, contact points and relations that must continue. A hand holding a tool, an eye directed at the object and a prop crossing a camera cut express a persistent relation, not just animated pixels. A flattened A cannot reveal the unseen back, a rig or hidden object geometry. Use a different viewpoint or an appropriate real asset rather than inventing those parts.

**From movement to a first-time reading.** A scene can be understood through what the eye can follow: action, consequence, hold, change of framing, words and silence. Time can illustrate sequence, comparison, uncertainty or cause; do not force every adjacent shot into a causal chain. If voice or music matters, use meaningful action/word cues rather than arbitrary timestamps, while allowing silent videos when appropriate.

**From render inspection to learning.** A still can expose geometry, a moving clip pacing, an encoded frame an export issue. These observations can refine the nearest method without creating an approval ritual. A model's assessment “as a first-time viewer” is not evidence of actual audience comprehension. Preserve that distinction.

The learned faculty is **situated perceptual translation**: understanding a source, forming an observable visual reference, and deriving a reproducible temporal expression that keeps the subject's meaning. Code and runtime decisions remain with the technical owner; no source project's code, assets or styles are bundled here.

## Prepare animatable elements from the reference

Before constructing motion, identify which image elements need to move separately and which can remain one asset.

The first genuinely generated and inspected **image A remains the visual reference**. It may establish art direction, the relation between characters/objects and the readable whole. But a single flattened illustration cannot independently animate a hand, held tool, background, eye movement or the reverse view of an object. When the actual motion needs them, form **additional source-bound visual elements, poses, masks or geometry**; when a single whole image suffices, keep it whole. Additional assets should remain coherent with the subject and A, while their provenance and roles stay identifiable. They are not retroactively relabelled as the first A.

This distinction was learned from [Generative Illustration](https://github.com/iart-ai/generative-illustration-skills), whose working methods separate illustrations into independently animable units. One of that project's source documents cautions that naïve SVG background removal can delete a foreground object. The lesson is to **preserve the subject when isolating parts**, not import a particular algorithm, template or vector file.

[HyperFrames media-use](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) also teaches that reused assets require source identity and separation across projects. An asset belongs to its context; a vaguely similar cached logo or illustration does not acquire the right identity. For a new video receiver, preserve the provenance/role needed to use media correctly, without assuming HyperFrames' own file manifest.

**What remains editable matters.** A scene translated into native editable elements, hosted time-seekable code or a finished rendered clip preserves different editing abilities. The rendered clip is not the same as a freely movable character. [Remotion ThreeCanvas](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion) is one example of 3D tied to absolute frame time, if that renderer is actually chosen.

**Format and language belong before the render.** A 16:9 master often must be **recomposed**, not merely cropped, for 9:16 while preserving people, objects and titles. Exact words should remain editable and, where appropriate, accessible through captions or transcripts, not be guessed from pixels in A. Current social platform overlay dimensions need case-specific confirmation.

Preserve the provenance and rights of the assets and code actually used in the project.

## Before and beyond the film: concept, non-linear relation, real asset transfer

These relationships can change the representation before a renderer is chosen.

**A media concept can precede the clip.** When an existing editorial or product concept already has a source-grounded core, a short video is one possible expression of that core alongside a still infographic, presentation or interactive guide. Recover the source, dominant relation, real-world consequence and audience before forming the image A prompt; do not derive the claim solely from a prior clip's visual treatment. Other derivatives preserve the thesis, not the same crop. A new A should still be actually generated and observed when this image-first workflow is selected.

**Linear playback does not make the subject linear.** A video can show co-presence, alternative interpretations, contrast, a recursive return that changes future possibilities, or present capability beside an open future horizon. Do not silently turn adjacency of shots into cause and effect, or a loop into an identical return. The appropriate temporal grammar emerges from the **kind of relation**, not from the number of seconds.

**Presentation and cognitive navigation are different capabilities.** A video may introduce a system through space, action and consequence, and share a visual identity with an interactive guide. The MP4 itself cannot accept selections, maintain live state or perform operations. Preserve that boundary when deriving other media from the same concept.

**Receiving an image is not receiving its description.** If one host generates A and another builds code scene B, the coding host must be able to **access the original image bytes or a genuinely accessible asset**, and know what was observed in it. A prompt, screenshot shown only in another chat or link to an instruction file does not complete that handoff. Use the existing storage/attachment/connector channel when available; do not invent an always-on bridge or require a universal metadata schema.

**Screenshot-based demos are not necessarily live product demonstrations.** An animated cursor can illustrate a possible flow, but does not establish that the UI click or automated result occurred in a running product. Distinguish captured screen, illustrative reconstruction and observed execution where the difference affects the claim.

These are reusable **perceptual and capability distinctions**, not copied code or mandatory preproduction stages.

## Image A precedes the coding, not necessarily frame zero

The first generated visual reference A is made and inspected **before programming the animation**, but it need not depict the video at `t=0`. It can be a key visual, a recognizable character, the most revealing action, a spatial relation or a representative moment. The coded scene B has its **own** opening state, and the useful relation to A may become visible later in the film. Do not force a pixel-identical first frame merely because A was generated first in the work.

**One represented situation, many visible projections.** Where the subject contains actions or changes of state, actors, held objects, camera views, text and sound should represent **consistent aspects of the same source-bound situation**. A click, gesture or change of viewpoint is not automatically evidence that an event actually occurred. Some films can be rendered from a simple shared relationship; others may benefit from a more elaborate model of actors, events and scene states. No formal state machine is universally required.

**A data variation is not always a different subject.** A reusable scene can render new values or variants when the underlying relationship remains appropriate. A truly different case may require a new perspective, visual grammar, source interpretation or initial image prompt rather than merely swapping names and colours in an old template. Preserve the *way of forming* scenes as reusable knowledge, not the scene geometry as a universal style.

## A video must be addressable in time

The export scene should support `frame(t, scene, assets)` or an equivalent absolute-frame method that can render frames **out of order**. GSAP tickers and Three.js `update(dt)` are live-web mechanisms, not an assurance of deterministic video. Seed randomness, wait for images/fonts/GPU to be ready, and preserve identities between shots. 30s × 24fps = 720 frames, but neither format nor frame rate is universal.

Separate **source time** (real recorded events and intervals) from **presentation time** (the timeline of the explanatory animation). When only the latter exists, the output is an explanatory short rather than documented time-lapse footage.

Audio and caption cues should share time data with the film if they contribute meaning. Silence can be the correct result. Check actual encoded duration, dimensions and track presence plus representative decoded frames when those claims matter; observing a clip is not proof of viewer understanding.

## Reusable recursive return

An image may introduce a false hierarchy; a character may lose its identity while turning; an HTML animation may fail to seek; a caption or soundtrack may create a misleading temporal implication. Correct the source interpretation, image instruction, visual design, code, sound or receiver method **at the owner that must act differently next time**. If a method already existed but was not reached, improve discovery, not the number of skill files. No claim that an exported film changes model weights or self-installs tools.

## MKCV — Mini Kernel Code Video, beyond the thirty-second profile

**MKCV** names the composition of competences for producing coded films. **VC-30s** identifies a short-duration profile. The stations in this guide can contribute to films with different durations, formats and visual languages when their subject calls for them.

**Public operating method:** use these instructions with the actual tools in your environment. Keep the generated and observed image A, editable scene B and exported film so the work can be resumed and improved.

## A production studio, not a software catalog

A short film can require different faculties: source understanding, visual direction, generated reference, people and objects, spatial/temporal grammar, code, speech and audio, rendering, delivery and learning. **Each station contributes situated know-how** and returns its causal learning to the nearest semantic owner. These faculties can work in one assistant or across multiple receivers with actual file access. The document alone does not invoke them.

Stations are **available when materially relevant**, not eleven mandatory phases in a fixed sequence. An image may be the right final result; voice may shape the storyboard before it is settled; viewing the generated image may change the source interpretation.

## Eleven competent production stations

| Station / competence | Know-how used during production | Contribution to the shared case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source and concept** — domain/editorial | Distinguishes observed facts, interpretation, illustration and future possibility; identifies the subject and audience | A source-grounded film nucleus |
| **Art direction** — perceptual composition | Selects visual relations, material, light, frame, space and depth from meaning rather than presets | Direction for generated reference and readable hierarchy |
| **Image A** — image-reference formation | Forms a source-bound prompt, generates a real image using an available generator, observes it | Accessible A asset, origin and what it teaches; A need not be film `t=0` |
| **Actors and assets** — visual continuity | Discerns needed independently moving elements, poses, props, layers, masks and actual geometry | Consistent identity and genuinely available resources |
| **Storyboard and time** — temporal direction | Distinguishes cause, co-presence, contrast, return and sequential change | Scene relation, beats/cues, world and camera continuity |
| **Coded scene B and animation** — code medium | Builds an editable 2D/2.5D/3D scene that can be rendered at time T | The actual coded first frame B, time-addressable movement |
| **Words and voice** — editorial expression | Holds exact claims/values and readable, editable captions, narration timing and accessibility | Spoken/on-screen text, or justified silence |
| **Sound** — audio craft | Forms sound events, atmosphere and music when they matter; recognizes rights and timing | Cue/mix/tracks or an audio-free result |
| **Render and inspect** — technical/perceptual readback | Exports through a real renderer and inspects frames and video files | Actually existing output and consequential corrections |
| **Delivery and derivatives** — channel competence | Adapts formats, subtitle and editability; recomposes layouts when crops destroy meaning | Actual carrier for the selected receiver/destination |
| **Learning and continuity** — meta competence | Distinguishes a completed artifact from knowledge that should change later work | Returned method, updated case and next point of reentry |

## Animate the reference’s subject

Clarify the requested movement: animate the character and objects depicted in the reference, construct a model from that image or create a new scene from the same concept. The three requests may need different assets and methods.

When the intended medium is image-to-motion, identify **a gesture within A** before drawing a new explanatory layer. technical production may use masks, 2.5D layers, coherent additional assets, restoration behind moving regions, an actual 3D model if needed, or image-to-video synthesis by a connected provider. A flattened A is not a hidden rig or a guarantee of temporal consistency. Diagrams and captions may help when they convey meaning, but must not replace the subject's movement.

**Optional technical receiver, not a dependency:** [Motion.so MCP](https://docs.motion.so/guides/mcp) documents agent-driven creation/revision, media uploads and video outputs. It does not prove that a given source image will be animated faithfully. It needs account permissions/OAuth and may consume credits; a local file must be uploaded before Motion can read it. The Motion product advertises social connections, but the documented MCP video tool list does **not itself establish direct social-posting tools**. Publication authority remains with the channel owner.

## Preserve identity and geometry in 2.5D animation

For limited movement, extract the head, arm or other relevant parts from the reference, define masks and pivots and reconstruct regions revealed by motion. Distinguish original pixels from reconstructed background. Light and camera should continue to describe the same setting.

Distinguish three production modes: animate the same image with 2.5D layers; construct 3D geometry from the reference; create a new scene entirely in code. The first serves limited motion and views. Free rotation, new surfaces and occlusion need the relevant geometry. Choose through the subject and requested effect.

The work drew **methods** from [cinetic](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/cinetic) (subject-owned movement and film direction), [img2threejs](https://github.com/img2threejs/img2threejs) (component inventory, hierarchy and unseen-part uncertainty), and [Remotion agent skills](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills) (composition versus rendered outcome). Neither their implementation code, stylistic constraints nor templates were imported.

Reconstructing eyelids or facial features can alter the character’s identity. Inspect the result and choose a smaller gesture when it preserves the subject better. Signs already present in the reference belong to the original scene; new panels or diagrams add another representational layer. For a different cinematic expression, form a new reference or coherent model.

## Operating from an actual case

Reach the **current source** and the material question first; do not activate all stations because they are listed.

For the image-first path, form instructions and **generate a real image A**, then inspect it. A can be a key visual, character, representative scene or important moment; being first **in the production process** does not mean showing frame zero **of the film**. When the action needs independent movable parts, acquire only the necessary additional assets coherent with A and the source, rather than imagining unseen body parts or 3D rigs.

Let the **type of relation** determine temporal expression. Build coded scene B with its real opening state and a renderer that can address time T (or an equivalent frame index) independently from uncontrolled browser timing. A simulated cursor over screenshots is an illustrative walkthrough, not evidence of an observed product action.

If image A and scene B are handled in different environments, the coding receiver must **actually access A's file bytes**. Merely providing a prompt, a text description or a skill link does not transfer the visual reference. Audio files, fonts and provider rights/credentials likewise require actual availability.

Inspect the delivered output as an artifact: read file metadata for duration, codec and dimensions; see representative frames/real playback to understand motion and legibility. Internal inspection is not proof that a real audience has understood the film.

## Minimal production case record

Create this **only after a case is selected**; retain just what changes continuation:

- **Subject:** source/version, intended audience and factual status (observed, reconstructed, allegorical, possible).
- **Active competences:** why each participated, real inputs/outputs and responsible owner.
- **A:** prompt, generated reference file actually reachable, provenance, observations and limits.
- **B:** editable scene, first coded frame, available assets, meaningful temporal model.
- **Output:** real film/other carrier, measured technical details and any actual distribution state.
- **Learning:** causal difference to apply in another case, owner whose future method changes, path/receipt and next state.

This is a continuity method, not a mandatory form. Keep private/client media and secrets in authorized locations.

**MKCV · beta, learning through use.** Mini Kernel Code Video is an evolving method. The examples explain how to work; observations of the film and corrections improve production and the knowledge for the next assignment.

A film’s shared continuation preserves its subject, source status, current artifact and the question that can change it. Each specialist owns the knowledge and changes that belong to its work. A staging discovery may change the written scene; an edit may request another performance; an unavailable joint may change the intended gesture. ## Optional technical example: renderer from an existing image

```python
"""A technical render example, not a semantic time-lapse storyboard.

Make the reference image with an image generator first and save it as a local PNG.
The real project's semantic transformation must be designed from its sources.
"""
from __future__ import annotations
import argparse
import shutil
import subprocess
from pathlib import Path
from PIL import Image, ImageOps


def frame_at(reference: Image.Image, t: float, duration: float, size: tuple[int, int]) -> Image.Image:
    """Render a code-built frame, using the generated image as a visible reference."""
    w, h = size
    u = max(0.0, min(1.0, t / duration))
    # Mild camera movement shows the rendering contract. Replace with a
    # source-grounded transformation; motion alone does not imply learning.
    zoom = 1.00 + 0.06 * (u * u * (3 - 2 * u))
    enlarged = ImageOps.fit(reference, (round(w * zoom), round(h * zoom)), method=Image.Resampling.LANCZOS)
    left = round((enlarged.width - w) * (0.45 + 0.1 * u))
    top = round((enlarged.height - h) / 2)
    return enlarged.crop((left, top, left+w, top+h)).convert('RGB')


def main() -> None:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(description='Reference-image to coded-frame rendering example')
    parser.add_argument('reference', type=Path, help='Image generated first using an image generator')
    parser.add_argument('--preview', type=Path, default=Path('first_frame_in_code.png'))
    parser.add_argument('--video', type=Path, default=None)
    parser.add_argument('--duration', type=float, default=30.0)
    parser.add_argument('--fps', type=int, default=24)
    parser.add_argument('--width', type=int, default=960)
    parser.add_argument('--height', type=int, default=540)
    args = parser.parse_args()
    if not args.reference.is_file(): parser.error('Provide an existing image created with the image generator')
    if args.duration <= 0 or args.fps <= 0 or args.width <= 0 or args.height <= 0:
        parser.error('Duration, fps and dimensions must be positive')
    if args.video and not shutil.which('ffmpeg'):
        parser.error('FFmpeg is required for MP4 export')
    reference = ImageOps.exif_transpose(Image.open(args.reference)).convert('RGB')
    size = (args.width, args.height)
    first = frame_at(reference, 0.0, args.duration, size)
    first.save(args.preview)
    if not args.video:
        print(f'Wrote coded first frame: {args.preview}')
        return
    total_frames = round(args.duration * args.fps)
    cmd = ['ffmpeg','-hide_banner','-loglevel','error','-y','-f','rawvideo','-pix_fmt','rgb24',
           '-s',f'{args.width}x{args.height}','-r',str(args.fps),'-i','-','-an',
           '-c:v','libx264','-pix_fmt','yuv420p',str(args.video)]
    encoder = subprocess.Popen(cmd, stdin=subprocess.PIPE, stderr=subprocess.PIPE)
    try:
        assert encoder.stdin is not None
        for i in range(total_frames):
            encoder.stdin.write(frame_at(reference, i/args.fps, args.duration, size).tobytes())
    except (BrokenPipeError, Exception):
        encoder.kill()
        encoder.communicate()
        raise
    else:
        assert encoder.stdin is not None
        encoder.stdin.close()
        assert encoder.stderr is not None
        err = encoder.stderr.read().decode('utf-8', errors='replace')
        code = encoder.wait()
        if code:
            raise RuntimeError(f'FFmpeg exit {code}: {err}')
    print(f'Wrote {total_frames} frames: {args.video}')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

```
