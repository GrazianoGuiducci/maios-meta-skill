# Worked examples

Illustrative cases for adaptation.

## Animate the world in the reference

A generated illustration shows a robot beside a worktable. The entrusted film asks that world to move. Adding a new overlay of boxes would make a different film. Observe the actual reference, identify the relevant head, arm, prop and environment, then determine which layers, pivots or geometry are available. A restrained 2.5D articulation may preserve identity better than an invented full 3D body. If hidden surfaces matter, form them as an explicit reconstruction or choose another view. The method follows the actual intended motion.

## A museum explores rhythm with three illustrations

A museum has three drawings of a tool and its use. Order, durations and points of attention suffice for an initial explanation: AI forms an animatic from the existing images, observes it and revises the timing of the discovery. If visitors need to understand how a lever moves, panning over the figure is insufficient: animatable parts or footage of the gesture become pertinent. The medium changes through what the audience needs to understand.

## A workshop edits a demonstration from existing footage

A workshop has filmed preparation, assembly and operation of an object. AI observes the clips, connects the steps and edits an explanation from actual footage. A time jump stays recognizable; adjacent images do not invent a cause. The project supplies clips and an editing tool. Observing the finished video may call for another cut or a missing view, rather than a new generated reference.

## An interrupted illustrative scene resumes its own timeline

A project has an original coded orbital scene and a fixed 72-frame timeline. After one 12-frame segment, production stops. The same job resumes: it decodes the completed segment, checks its identity and renders the remaining intervals. A changed job is kept separate; a damaged segment is quarantined and rebuilt. The completed local file is checked as a whole. This example teaches technical continuation; the scene’s orbit is illustrative and its on-screen labels are the original Italian artwork, not scientific observation or a customer result.
