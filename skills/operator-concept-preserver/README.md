# Operator Concept Preserver

**Extract an author's ideas from AI conversations**

[Italiano](README_IT.md) · Public edition 0.3.0 · English operating method

## What it does

Separates the author's words from assistant output, reduces noise and duplication, and organises useful concepts with source references.

## When to use it

Use it when knowledge is scattered across chats, notes or transcripts and you want ideas or methods you can reuse.

This competence recovers concepts, corrections and insights expressed by the author from a selected corpus of chats, notes or transcripts. It keeps the source accessible and distinguishes what the person expressed from what the assistant developed.
It works on the corpus actually selected. The result makes ideas usable for research, a project or writing without turning a model's summary into the author's voice.

## First use

1. Copy or attach [START_CHAT_EN.txt](START_CHAT_EN.txt). It includes the English method, operating knowledge, worked examples and any supplied executable sources.
2. Add your actual project, intent, relevant sources, existing work and available tools.
3. Entrust the useful contribution and identify actions the assistant may perform. Preserve its guide for later reentry.

You can begin with: “Use this competence for this work: […]. These are the sources and available means: […]. Carry out the entrusted work and retain what will let it continue. Involve me where […].” Adapt this to knowledge already present; it is not a compulsory questionnaire.

## Method

### Understand the corpus and its purpose

Identify which documents, conversations, periods and people belong to the requested work. Understand whether the aim is ideas for a text, requirements, corrections to a method or a reconstruction of concepts. Use the materials available in the authorised environment; a link or the name of a chat is not equivalent to having read its content. If a part is missing, state the coverage and continue where the material is sufficient. Do not extend the analysis to personal archives that were not selected.

### Attribute the words before deriving the concept

Recognise the author, other participants and the type of passage. Separate the person's words, assistant summaries, quoted text, tool messages and attached material. A simple acknowledgement does not automatically make the model's entire previous response the author's contribution. Preserve the reference that allows the passage to be reopened: file and position, identifier or date when available. Read the context needed to understand what a correction refers to; do not attribute the latest formulation to a time when it was not yet present.

### Reduce noise while preserving meaning

Group genuine duplication and leave logs, navigation, routine confirmations and unnecessary confidential data out of the working result. Do not rewrite sources to clean them up. Repetition may clarify a concept or change its meaning: preserve that difference instead of deduplicating only by similarity. An unfinished insight may deserve to remain open; the absence of a final formulation does not authorise you to complete it with a thesis of your own.

### Form a result that can be checked

For each useful concept, make its meaning, source, the situation it addresses and possible use recognisable. Keep a short quotation when the wording is decisive and separate it from your paraphrase. Explicitly attribute interpretations, connections and possible developments proposed by the assistant. Distinguish a personal preference, a project fact and a generalisable method when this changes what follows; do not assign levels or importance through a fixed system. Themes may intersect without becoming closed categories.

### Let ideas continue in the relevant place

A correction can change a competence, a requirement can enter the project and an insight can inform a draft. Connect each return to the part that will use it. A collection of ideas does not entail publication, rewriting the original or approval of derived content. When a quotation contains private details, preserve the source in its own environment and prepare only the portion needed for the selected work. The ability to attribute contributions must also accompany extracts passed to another assistant.

## Meta and autological relations

The direct work is attributed extraction. A meta relation appears when a correction recovered from the corpus changes how other competences write, research or are formed.

The relation is autological when extraction recognises that its own method was replacing the author's voice: for example, by attributing a model-generated generalisation to the author. It then corrects both the extract and the attribution criterion. Reconstruction remains limited to sources actually available.

## Use in your environment

### In a chat

Copy or attach START_CHAT_EN.txt and add your working context. It includes the complete method in English. Keep the useful return for the next session; the assistant may converse in your language.

### In an agentic environment

Make the resource folder readable to the assistant, point it to SKILL.md, and choose the project documents that will carry continuity. Tools and actions depend on the actual environment and mandate.

### With or without a kernel

With an existing kernel, connect the method to the relevant competence and guides. Without one, the complete starter supplies the operating method for the work described here.

## Application examples

- Gather ideas for a book from conversations selected by its author.
- Extract product requirements from project conversations.
- Recover corrections that should change an assistant's working method.

[Read the examples](EXAMPLES.md). Cases illustrate possible use; they are not evidence of a recipient’s deployment.

## Learning and continuation

Keep coverage decisions, references and ambiguities that change the reading in the work guide. A correction by the author can update the extract without rewriting what was said earlier.
Return the knowledge to the competence that needs to act differently. A well-referenced collection makes it possible to check and use concepts; it does not by itself certify completeness, absolute originality or assimilation.
