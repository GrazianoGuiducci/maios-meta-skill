# Worked examples

Illustrative cases. Conditions, decisions and continuation are supplied to teach the method; these are not reports of customer results.

## Changing order changes the discovery

Three illustrative shots show a bent plant, a character noticing it and a hand supporting it. In that order the viewer discovers the need, the attention and the action. Start with the completed support and the same material becomes a reveal of how it happened. Both may work for different intentions. Choose through the film’s question, then preserve the reason. The sequence on a resource page demonstrates this editing relation; it does not document a real autonomous robot.


## A juxtaposition invents a cause

A report contains a worker leaving a room and a machine stopping later, with no causal relationship established. Cutting directly between those images can suggest responsibility. Recover what the sources actually support and recompose order, context or point of view. If the intended film is fiction, the author can instead deliberately create that relationship. The change belongs to the represented object, not merely to transition style.


## Sound opens the next space

A quiet workshop shot is followed by an outdoor scene. Introducing the outdoor sound before the cut can make the next space anticipated; carrying workshop sound over the new image can preserve a different continuity. Try the relation with actual sound or a clearly provisional cue and observe what the viewer can infer. If silence carries the intended transition better, retain it. A sound bridge is a useful possibility rather than a required track.
