# Film Montage

Forms cinematic meaning through the temporal relation of shots, duration, sound and transitions, preserving the source and the scene’s intended consequences.

Two images can imply a relation that neither shows alone. Montage understands that relationship while forming the film, from a rough reel to the final cut.

The work can concern continuity, comparison, surprise, silence or a sustained shot. Its form follows the actual subject and the viewer’s possible discovery.

## Understand what the sequence should make knowable

Recover the source, authored situation, intended point of view and current footage or scene model. Identify what a viewer can discover through the encounter between shots. A timeline can imply succession, comparison, cause or omission. Preserve the distinction between an observed event and a meaning authored by the edit.


## Form the relationship through order and duration

Choose when a shot begins and ends, what remains long enough to be perceived and what is omitted. A held shot or continuous take may be the right temporal form. Rhythm follows the action and discovery; it is not a fixed cut frequency. Compare how an entrance or exit changes the reading of a gesture or reaction.


## Use continuity and sound as situated tools

Eyeline, action matches and spatial orientation can keep a world intelligible; a deliberate discontinuity can create another relationship. Sound may start before the next image or continue after the previous one, changing expectation and information. Silence can do meaningful work. Choose these means when the source and scene need them, with actual available audio and rights.


## Observe the inference created by a cut

If juxtaposition creates an unsupported cause, authority or emotional attribution, return to the source and recompose with writing and direction. A fluent transition can conceal a false relationship. In fiction the edit can author a connection; documentary or technical explanation needs that connection qualified by its source. Preserve the distinction in the final expression.


## Compose an actual reel and learn from it

A rough assembly can expose missing reaction, unnecessary repetition or an impossible transition before final rendering. Let the observation change writing, direction or performance through their owners. Production implements the timeline, seeks, compositing and encoding with real means. Preserve the reason for the edit and the reusable temporal knowledge for another film.

## Continue through the actual project

Keep the intended relation and observed effect of the cut, not only its timestamp.

Return temporal meaning here and encoding, reproducibility or tool learning to the production medium.
