# Film Montage

Edit images and sound to form a film’s meaning

Choose order, duration and transitions through what the sequence lets the viewer discover.

Forms cinematic meaning through the temporal relation of shots, duration, sound and transitions, preserving the source and the scene’s intended consequences.

When individual shots work but their order, rhythm or sound changes the film’s meaning.

## Adapt the method to your context

Montage can tell a choice in fiction, explain a procedure or connect testimony in a documentary. Preserve the relationship between order, duration, images, sound and understood meaning. Materials, audience, intent and the relationships supported by sources change.

Provide the available material and what the viewer should be able to understand. Compare sequences that materially change that understanding: a demonstration makes before and after readable; fiction may delay a discovery; a documentary preserves the distinction between adjacent events and causation. The plant case teaches this choice through three illustrative shots.
