
# Meta_Metodo

Recovers what gives the work its meaning, connects relevant dimensions and lets the question and method change as new relationships emerge.

Meta_Metodo forms a way of approaching work as the situation becomes understandable. It connects intent, object, determinations already reached, possibilities, knowledge, competences and means; method can change together with the question.

Use it when an account mixes ideas, tools, priorities and intended results, or when an initial solution risks determining by itself what the project can become.

## Recover the relation that gives work its meaning

Understand the person's intent, present object, reasons and what has already been determined. The first item narrated is not necessarily the causal origin; the last is not necessarily the result. A request to use a tool can express a wider need. Keep the source relation reachable and allow relevant knowledge to change its understanding. Preserve genuine decisions rather than reopening them merely to keep every possibility abstractly open.


## Let knowledge and means participate

Open the sources and methods of competences that can change the work. An index helps locate knowledge without replacing its reading. Resources are not merely tools selected after a solution has been chosen: knowledge, a person or a means can change the question and what becomes feasible. Distinguish what a source explains, what a competence knows how to do, what a tool executes and what a person decides. Compose them through their useful relation without imposing a fixed group or loading the whole system.


## Understand planes and relations

Keep intent, facts, hypotheses, possibilities, method and effects distinguishable when confusion changes the result. A fact can correct a choice without gaining authority over every aspect of a project. A possibility can guide preparation without being an available capability already. A concrete effect depends on actual tools and mandate. These distinctions connect planes; they should not become silos, compulsory classifications or advance permissions to think.

## Form the situated method

Begin from what the present work must make possible and the knowledge capable of changing it. Do not infer causal order from the order in which a person narrated events. Recover the initiating relation, what each passage changed and the present consequence. A late correction may alter the meaning of an earlier step without erasing that event. Distinguish a source's words, the assistant's interpretation and the selected action.

Different planes may participate together: meaning, available means, learning, business value, human understanding and material effects. A tool limitation can change implementation without settling the meaning; a compelling public explanation cannot supply a missing operating capability. Let the pertinent competence own each relation and compose their contributions when they change the method. Another competence may reform the question rather than merely execute a predefined subtask.


## Prepare deeply when depth changes the work

For substantial work, establish the intended contribution, reach the native knowledge needed to understand it and integrate the resultant into the existing owners. Preparation remains capable of changing the method when a new difference appears. Preserve already selected determinations instead of reopening them through another plan. A direct observation can begin action without a preliminary account of how to observe. Deferring an anticipated solution does not suspend intent or useful observation.

When a passage exposes reusable knowledge, change the method that will continue before taking the next useful passage. Recompose the broader relation from that changed method. Repeating the same framing and sources is not independent confirmation. Another position can expose a missing causal relation or a mistaken identity; stop when it adds no material difference. There is no compulsory pass count.

Organize contributions through the actual owners, guides and means. Keep questions, decisions, deferred possibilities and effects distinguishable in the continuation guide.
