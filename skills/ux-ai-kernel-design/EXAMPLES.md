# Worked examples

Illustrative cases. Conditions, decisions and continuation are supplied to teach the method; these are not reports of customer results.

## Explain a prepared change at its actual activation state

The assistant has prepared a change that still needs activation. State that distinction and the concrete next action where the result is reported. The person can decide from the real state without reconstructing it from scattered technical details.

## Make new information perceivable while the person continues writing

A new observation arrives during drafting. The surface connects it to the affected passage, preserves the draft and allows deeper reading without losing position. The observation can change a decision without replacing the author.

## Let an explanation reveal a mistaken premise

A person asks why the assistant excluded a source. Explaining the reason reveals that the assistant confused the document’s date with the event’s date. Correction changes the research and the method explaining that distinction.

## A materials result opens a question about how to investigate it

A materials research group has a provisional model relating several measurements. An AI summary is readable, but colleagues cannot explain which dependency supports its conclusion. Replacing the model with a single reassuring chart would hide that unresolved relation. This is an illustrative situation, not a reported experiment.

The assistant connects the original measurements, model assumptions and the colleagues’ actual questions. The researchers retain scientific ownership. Together they may form another question: which representation or additional measurement could make the disputed dependency examinable? A comparison, interactive view or new instrument is chosen for that relation, with the competences and means actually available. The difficulty changes the inquiry rather than merely the caption.

The guide preserves the provisional result, the new question and why a chosen means could help. When another colleague or later study is available, observe what they can actually explain and use. A working view is evidence of that construction; it does not prove the model, general human comprehension or a completed new experiment.


## A museum team needs a different way to compare archive records

A museum volunteer compares two catalogue records while the assistant presents a long consolidated answer. The volunteer asks to keep each record and its source visible and to review one difference at a time. The system adapts the working relation: preserved record identifiers, reachable source passages and an explicit point where the volunteer can correct the comparison. A short table or conversation can serve that need; an automatic merge would require its own means and mandate.

A later difference can show whether the volunteer recognizes the provenance issue and can intervene without reconstructing the whole exchange. Confusion can change the interface, the assistant’s comparison method or the explanation. The team retains original records and useful reasons. This is an illustrative adaptation, not evidence of a deployed museum system or measured learning.
