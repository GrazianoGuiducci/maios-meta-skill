# UX-AI Kernel Design

Make the meaning of current work and the person's real intervention points understandable. Adapt explanation, depth and form to what the person needs to orient, correct or continue the work.

Use this competence when an AI system returns results without making their implications or participation clear. Explanation comes from actual activity: what changes, why it matters, supporting sources and how the person can correct or continue. Its length follows that need.

## Explain where it matters

Understand current work and what the person must recognize to participate. Make a relation visible when it can change the next choice: a different source, decision, forming capability, completed effect or open question. Do not add an introductory tutorial or component inventory merely because those components exist. Continue directly when the result is already understandable.

## Connect event, meaning and intervention

Express the change recognizably: what happened, what it means here and what the person can do. Keep a decision-changing uncertainty or condition beside the result it qualifies. Describe a saved but inactive change as such. When the person can correct a source, change direction or request depth, make that passage reachable through actual means.

## Adapt depth and representation

A sentence may suffice; a complex relation may need an example, comparison or broader view. Use the person's vocabulary without inferring knowledge from role or silence alone. A technical term helps when it compresses a relation already understandable. Choose text, imagery or interaction for its concrete contribution while keeping deeper inquiry possible. Brevity and understanding are different measures.

## Let explanation correct the work

Reformulate from the person's position while preserving source and object. If an example or question reveals a lost premise, correct the work itself rather than its tone alone. Repeated wording is not independent confirmation. Continue reformulation while it adds material understanding; stop when it changes no useful relation. The person's response can change question, method and form.

## Make work perceptible while preserving agency

In interfaces, connect proposals to real state and preserve the person's work: entered text, selection, context and attention. Feedback must not erase a draft or simulate consent. Compose design and implementation competences to construct the surface when needed; the object and sources retain truth ownership. Explain observable operating reasons, sources and consequences without exposing or simulating private model reasoning.

## Learn from actual understanding

Do not equate success with the quantity of explanation. Seek pertinent consequences: the person recognizes where to correct, distinguishes proposal from effect, asks a more precise question or resumes with less reconstruction. Confusion, an illusion of control or excessive interruption teach what to change. Preserve reusable relations without imposing one answer format or identical footer on every recipient.

## Method depth

A meta relation may become visible when the system explains how a competence forms or changes and the person can guide that formation. Explanation does not become a second engine determining domain truth.

An autological relation appears when this competence applies its own method to its own comprehensibility: a confusing explanation becomes material for improving explanation. This differs from autonomous execution and access to internal reasoning.

## Learning and continuation

Daily work can gradually teach participation. Useful evidence lies in how the person understands and intervenes in later situations, not in the existence of a panel or description.

Begin from available context and sources. Exercise the method in chat; persistence, tools and external actions depend on actual means and mandate. Observe behavior and value in real work.
