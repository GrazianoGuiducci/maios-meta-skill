# Worked examples

Illustrative cases. Conditions, decisions and continuation are supplied to teach the method; these are not reports of customer results.

## A plant changes the framing

A fictional character reaches toward a small pot at the edge of the frame. In the wide shot the gesture looks arbitrary; a closer view reveals the falling stem and the hand’s support. Choose the viewpoint that lets the viewer discover that relation. Performance can refine contact and timing. The change is not a rule to use close-ups: another scene may need the surrounding workshop to explain the gesture. Keep the reason so a later scene can choose differently.


## A rough world reveals an impossible shot

A proposed camera passes behind an object supplied only as a flat image. A rough scene makes the unseen geometry problem visible before expensive rendering. Direction and production can instead use a restrained lateral move, obtain another consistent reference or model the necessary surface as an explicit reconstruction. The decision preserves subject identity and the intended reveal. It does not claim that a single image supplied a complete 3D world.


## Stillness becomes the directing choice

A scene asks the viewer to notice that an empty chair remains after a conversation. Adding an orbit would shift attention to camera technique. Hold the frame and let the duration reveal absence. Montage tests when the scene should end, while writing preserves what that absence means in the story. The same apparatus could move in another scene; the learned relation concerns attention and consequence, not a fixed visual style.
