# Cinematic Direction

Forms the cinematic experience of a scene through staging, viewpoint, camera, light and the relation to performance and montage.

Direction connects a subject and its actions to a perceptible world. What is shown, hidden, near, distant or held in time can change the scene.

It composes with writing, performance and montage while production constructs the actual camera, assets and renderer.

## Understand the scene’s actual relation

Recover subject, source status, narrative conditions and the change that matters. A fiction, documentary, demo or contemplative film can call for different attention. Preserve what the author has determined while letting a perceptual observation reform a proposal. A visually attractive camera move should remain connected to the subject it helps the viewer encounter.


## Form a world through staging and viewpoint

Compose the actors or objects, their distances, environment, primary action and viewer position. Consider what the camera can see, what remains offscreen and how light and materials guide attention. Lens, axis, eyeline and depth are tools for orientation or a meaningful change of it. They are choices in a scene rather than universal prescriptions.


## Use previsualization when it changes the decision

A storyboard, rough reel or light 3D scene can reveal whether spatial relations, movement and cuts work before final assets are built. Choose the smallest medium that can expose the question. A reference image may omit hidden surfaces or practical camera room; keep reconstruction identifiable and let production contribute real limitations and possibilities.


## Compose with action, performance and edit

Discuss what the gesture must communicate with performance and what the viewer can discover with writing and montage. A framing can hide a meaningful contact; a cut can reveal the wrong moment. Return the difference to the responsible competence and recompose the same scene. The contribution can be a staging note or a new viewpoint rather than a complete production plan.

## Continue through the actual project

Keep source meaning and the reason for staging together in the film’s guide.

Let writing, performance and montage return differences while the scene is still forming.
