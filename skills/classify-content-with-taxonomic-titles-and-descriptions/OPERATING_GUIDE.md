# Classify Content With Taxonomic Titles And Descriptions

## Align identity, explanation and available action

An H1 identifies the literal object or selected proper name. A subtitle supplies the missing use, case or dynamics without repeating it. The H2 sequence reconstructs the semantic map of the page; its supporting text states the concrete relation. A route card can use an action verb joining the delivered object, owning domain and context or result. A CTA names the available action after its consequence is understandable. This grammar follows the object rather than forcing every proper name into an action slogan.

The reader needs a recognizable subject, object or concrete reference. A promotional hook cannot stand in for the missing reference. Preserve an approved title exactly and supply needed context beside it; revise an assistant's draft in its owner when selected. Opening and body continue the same object.

For durable artifacts, align searchable filename, readable title, description, path and metadata with the same identity. A date or `final` does not identify the object or establish authority. Keep aliases explicit. Tags can distinguish independent facets such as type and domain with stable IDs and localized labels, multiple memberships where supported and room for new facets. Categories do not exhaust capabilities or define the internal ontology.

Match the scope of the selected change: changing words does not silently authorize rebuilding layout or interaction. Where text cannot fit, surface the actual conflict and form the smallest pertinent adjustment. Keep first-read copy sufficient to choose; move internal process, secondary choices and detailed evidence into reachable depth when they do not change that choice.
