# Cinematic Screenwriting

Forms original cinematic situations, treatments, scenes and dialogue, preserving authorship and causal continuity while composing with direction, performance and montage.

A scene makes something happen in a world. Its meaning can come from a gesture, discovery, choice, silence or a relationship that changes.

This first public method carries source-grounded cinematic knowledge and reciprocal collaboration. Its usefulness grows through actual writing and observation of the resulting scenes.

## Find the situation and its status

Understand the subject, author’s intent and what kind of representation is entrusted: observed documentation, reconstruction, allegory or invented fiction. Identify the world, point of view and what may change. Fiction can author intentions and an inner life; a technical film needs sources for claims about real people, systems and events. Let the situation determine its narrative form.


## Distinguish a beat from a scene

A beat can be one discovery or choice. A scene makes it possible under the conditions of a world: what actors or objects know, can do and encounter, and what the viewer can understand. Form the conditions, action and change before translating them into screen time. A physical activity becomes narrative action through its relationship to the situation.


## Write what can become perceptible

Develop a treatment, scenes, actions, words or meaningful silence to the depth needed. Preserve the difference between what a character knows and what the viewer knows. Dialogue, subtext and omission should make something happen, not explain what the image already supplies. Do not require an antagonist, three-act structure or advertising formula when the object calls for another form.


## Let the other cinematic competences change the writing

Give direction the narrative conditions and change; performance the action and any authored intention; montage the relations the viewer should discover. Their spatial, bodily and temporal findings can reform the scene. Keep authored meaning with writing and actual executable means with production. A code renderer receives events and states; it does not become the author by filling missing meaning with effects.


## Keep continuity and learn from the scene

Observe whether the written situation survives an image, performance or assembled sequence. Keep identities, world facts and relationships that materially affect what happens. A failed scene can change the question as well as the wording. Return reusable dramaturgical knowledge to this method and specific plot or production decisions to the work’s own guide.

## Continue through the actual project

Preserve the author’s determinations; let observed consequences reform what remains open.

Keep narrative, perceptual, technical and project learning at their respective competences.
