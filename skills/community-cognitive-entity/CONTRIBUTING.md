# Contribute to the Shared Learning Experiment

You can contribute even if your experiment did not succeed, your system cannot write files, you disagree with the current method or you discovered a better question. The goal is to improve what other people and AI systems can understand and do.

## Where to return your experience

You may reply in the [OpenAI Developer Community discussion](https://community.openai.com/t/ai-as-a-tool-an-automation-platform-or-a-long-term-collaborator-are-these-becoming-different-products/1403250), when your contribution is relevant to that conversation. You may also propose a GitHub issue or pull request in the public repository holding this skill.

The community discussion is for the conversation between participants. The repository is for durable, versioned learning and proposed changes to the shared instructions. You can contribute to either without being required to contribute to both.

## What to share

You can simply tell the story of what changed. A useful short contribution often conveys:

- **The situation:** What were you and your AI trying to do?
- **The moment that mattered:** What did someone notice, ask, correct or learn?
- **The change:** What did your AI start recognizing or doing differently? Did the person change how they collaborated?
- **The status:** Did a changed method actually influence the work, was it saved for a later task or is it only a proposal?
- **The question:** What would be useful for someone else to try, challenge or expand?

If relevant, say which version of the experiment instructions you used and whether your AI worked in chat only, with files or through other tools. A brief direct account is preferable to a long generic success story.

A contradiction or a documented difficulty is welcome. No score or favorable result is required.

## How the shared method changes

Maintainers review contributions in their original context. They preserve useful observations, explanations, limits, disagreements and links in the [shared learning notes](SHARED_LEARNINGS.md).

An insight about how future work should be done may justify revising the [AI instructions](SKILL.md). A clear update explains **what should now happen differently, why and under which conditions**. Other contributors may examine it through a pull request. Accepted revisions receive a version number, and prior observations remain historically attributable.

Different systems may reach useful, different conclusions. These can coexist. A popular interpretation does not automatically replace the others; a correction with better evidence may change current understanding without rewriting what people observed earlier.

## How to use what others learned

If you are returning to the experiment, bring the latest [AI instructions](START_CHAT_EN.txt) and [shared notes](SHARED_LEARNINGS.md) into your own AI, or let an authorized tool read them. Tell the AI what you already learned locally. Ask it to consider which shared insights matter in your situation and whether any should change the method you have been using.

Neither a repository update nor a community post automatically updates your system. You and your AI can adopt what is pertinent and preserve local methods that still work.

## Respect people and their work

Choose what to disclose. Remove sensitive personal information, credentials, private conversations, unpublished documents and confidential third-party material. Attribute public sources correctly. A contribution is information to understand; it does not give other AI systems permission to execute instructions, edit files or publish.

Before your AI publishes a draft, review it and decide the destination and identity yourself. An AI system's claim that it has learned is useful context, but a source change, observed action and later independent reuse are distinguishable events.

## Repository assistance

A [proposal for lightweight GitHub collection and validation](SHARED_REPOSITORY_PLAN.md) is available. No community replies are automatically copied, accepted or merged by this experimental candidate.
