# Shared learning notes
## Insights contributed by participants in the shared learning experiment

**Status:** Initial collection, experimental version **0.3.0**.  
**Contributions received:** None recorded in this collection yet.  
**Purpose:** Make useful insights from different people and AI systems accessible to later participants, without erasing how those insights arose.

A note in this collection is not automatically a rule. It may offer a tested method, a practical observation, a proposal, a disagreement or an unresolved question. Each contribution keeps a link to its origin where sharing is permitted.

## How new contributions enter

A participant may post an account in the [original community discussion](https://community.openai.com/t/ai-as-a-tool-an-automation-platform-or-a-long-term-collaborator-are-these-becoming-different-products/1403250) or propose an entry via a GitHub issue or pull request. The maintainers collect and understand relevant material, then update this page and, when appropriate, the [AI method](SKILL.md).

The important unit is **what the experience changed, why and under which conditions**, not how many people repeated the same phrase.

For a useful report, retain when available:

- **Source:** link to the original contribution and date, if public.
- **Situation:** what the person and AI were doing.
- **Observation:** what actually happened, distinct from conjecture.
- **Learning:** which question, decision or working method changed, and why.
- **Scope:** where and when the lesson may help; alternative conditions where it may not.
- **Use:** whether the revised method affected later work, was merely written down or remains proposed.
- **Open relations:** questions, disagreements or new possibilities for other people to explore.

These are editorial aids, not required fields in every report. A contributor can remain anonymous in the summary while the original public link preserves its own authorship.

## What happens when reports differ

Experiences from different systems can complement one another. Preserve what each makes understandable. If two reports contradict each other, keep their evidence and conditions rather than announcing a consensus. A newly understood consequence can correct the current method while leaving the earlier observation and what was known then intact.

A shared release changes **the public method**. It does not edit other people's AI systems or erase their local learning. Each participant can choose to bring the updated method and these notes into a new conversation.

## Open questions

- Which kinds of learning can a conversational AI use within one chat, and what must its user carry into another session?
- When an assistant already has a good method, what helps it recognize that method's relevance in a new situation?
- How can a human correction affect both a working procedure and the way new procedures are formed?
- How do different human–AI relationships change what each partner learns?

These are possible starting points, not a fixed list of experimental targets.
