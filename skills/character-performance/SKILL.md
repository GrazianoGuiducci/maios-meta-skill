# Character & Object Performance

Forms the observable performance of animated characters and expressive objects, connecting identity, authored intention, action, reaction and scene.

A rig supplies movement. Performance understands what that movement communicates in a situation, including what becomes legible through stillness.

A character in fiction can have an authored intention. An animated image of a real system needs sources before attributing an inner state or autonomous decision.

## The robot’s gesture becomes an understandable reaction

In the fiction, a robot notices a fallen plant and helps it. If its arm moves immediately, the gesture can look like a mechanical cycle; a pause and gaze make the discovery readable.

Performance gives authored action an observable form through the body, timing and contact. Actual assets and joints determine how it can be constructed.

## Adapt the method to your context

Performance animates a fictional character, an object reacting to weight or a gesture in a demonstration. Preserve the relationship between action, reaction and the signs an audience can read. Body, material, available joints and authorial intent change.

Provide the subject, gesture and limits of the animatable material. The plant’s robot is an invented character; a technical tutorial may instead need only pressure, contact and the object’s response. Hesitation and care belong to fiction when authored in the scene; explain a real machine’s movement through its documented operation.

## Understand the actor’s relationship to the scene

Recover what the actor or object is, what remains recognizable, what action is entrusted and what the situation allows. Receive any intention from the authored story. A demonstration object may need weight, balance or transformation without being humanized. Let the actual subject determine whether gaze, breath, contact or reaction is meaningful.


## Form the readable action

Connect observation, a narrated change when present, action, reaction and consequence. Choose a silhouette, pose, balance, gaze or contact that lets the viewer perceive the relation. A pause can make a discovery visible before the body moves. The goal is not to put motion everywhere but to let each relevant change carry the scene.


## Distinguish time, spacing and intensity

Significant key poses can support variation within a pose, anticipation, secondary action, overlap and follow-through when useful. Timing concerns when and how long; spacing concerns the change between positions; intensity changes the impression of the gesture. They can work together without becoming the same parameter. Test meaningful moments and the continuous movement with the actual medium.


## Work through the available body and assets

A single image does not provide every joint, surface or expression. Compose with production to identify layers, masks, pivots, rigs or geometry that can actually support the gesture. Preserve identity through reconstructed parts. Change the performance or viewpoint when the asset cannot carry it. A technical turn of a head is movement; its relation to an observed object makes it performance.


## Compose and return the difference

Direction makes the gesture visible; writing preserves its narrative meaning; montage determines which reaction can be perceived; code implements actual motion. Let a finding reform the scene through its owner. Preserve reusable performance learning here and technical rig learning with production. A later non-identical scene can show which knowledge has become useful.
