# Worked examples

Illustrative cases. Conditions, decisions and continuation are supplied to teach the method; these are not reports of customer results.

## The robot sees before reaching

In a fictional scene a robot helps a fallen plant. If the arm reaches instantly, the action reads as a programmed mechanical cycle. A brief gaze toward the pot, a change of pose and a later reach make the authored discovery legible. Keep the movement proportionate to the story and available joints. This is an animated interpretation, not evidence that a real AI feels concern or internally planned the action. A simpler scene may omit the gaze and convey the same relation through contact and framing.


## An object can have weight without a face

A film shows a heavy block settling onto a surface. The subject needs mass, balance and contact, not eyes or a personality. A slower initial response and small settling motion may make its material relation readable. Direction provides the view where contact is visible; production chooses suitable motion or simulation. The learned distinction is between expressive physical behavior and unnecessary humanization.


## A small gesture preserves identity

Only one image of a character is available. A reconstructed blink distorts the face, while a subtle head turn and pause preserve its recognizable features. Observe both results and keep the performance that carries the intended reaction. If the story truly needs the expression, obtain coherent additional assets or build the missing geometry. The decision follows the scene rather than assuming that a bitmap contains a complete face rig.
