# A guide to the project possibilities Radar

## The project the Radar starts from

The Radar understands the project’s purpose, current work, established decisions and what its people can do. It reads available sources and possibilities retained from previous cycles. A new tool can be relevant, as can an emerging need, an accessible resource or a changed way of using an existing capability.

## Sources and their connection to a concrete need

The AP searches and compares information through the means available to it. It distinguishes a source’s claims from what the project could do with them. For example, a cultural archive might encounter a text-recognition tool: the Radar clarifies which documents it supports, what work it could help with and which conditions remain to be understood.

## Possibilities the project receives and contributions it can offer

The connection can work in both directions. An external development can open a new use within the project; one of its competences can become useful to another organization. The Radar preserves the possible recipient, reasons, sources, required resources and following question. Follow-up may be an investigation, a deferred possibility or an informative handoff to whoever will develop the work.

## Possibilities over time and useful notifications

Possibilities remain in an archive that preserves reasons, conditions and changes. New information updates the same case when it concerns the same relationship. The AP can learn that a source was unsuitable or that a competence was missing from its reading and improve its method. Notifications follow the entrusted preferences; a cycle without useful differences can remain quiet.

## The project and the first Radar cycle

Name the project, its purpose, current work and available sources. Start with a single development or an earlier possibility if useful. Ask the Radar to clarify what could change, on which grounds and under which conditions. The observed field can evolve when a pertinent relationship emerges.

## Instructions to copy into your own LLM

Prepare and copy the complete starter from this page or use START_CHAT_EN.txt. Add context before or during the conversation: the text already contains the method, guide and examples. The assistant uses what it knows and can reach, then asks for pertinent missing information. Copying works without downloading the package.

## Sources, archive and competences in your environment

Make entrusted materials accessible. With persistent files, retain the AP folder and use ACTIVITY.md as its entry. The package includes four complete methods: Signal Intelligence qualifies sources and signals; Situated Possibility Formation understands connections and possibilities; Meta-Activity connects mandate and scheduling; Situated Activity Evolution improves usefulness and method through results. In a chat, retain and provide the continuity note again. Specify where the Radar will find possibilities and who could use them.

## Radar scheduling and notifications

After a first cycle, choose an event or cadence, timezone, recipient and notifications. You need an activity-management program, the controller, that can execute the assignment in your environment. Read back the instructions, status and schedule actually saved. Scheduling depends on the means available in your system. Choose whether to remain quiet when nothing useful changes.

## The result to read and the possibility’s follow-up

Check whether you can understand which relationship emerged, why it matters to the project and what is missing for continuation. A possibility can remain in the archive with its conditions or pass to whoever will perform distinct work. Sources, attribution and status stay recognizable; the guide’s cases are illustrative. Later cycles resume from the present and useful consequences.

## Origin and status of the Radar method

A MAIOS resource by Graziano Guiducci, composed from Signal Intelligence, Situated Possibility Formation, Meta-Activity and Situated Activity Evolution. This first edition transfers the Radar method with illustrative cases. Configuration, execution and results in the receiving system retain their own conditions and evidence.
